## AI Refresher

> *Conceptual scaffold — starting point before reading. Expand and refine from textbooks.*

Statistical mechanics is the bridge between the microscopic world (positions, momenta, interactions) and the macroscopic world (temperature, pressure, free energy). The central challenge is that while Newton's laws govern individual molecules, we don't want to know where every molecule is — we want pressure, temperature, and free energy. These emerge from averaging over microscopic chaos.

**The core idea — microstates and macrostates:**
At any instant, a system is in a *microstate* — a specific set of positions and momenta for every particle. Many different microstates can correspond to the same *macrostate* — the same temperature, pressure, and volume. Statistical mechanics tells us how to average over all microstates consistent with our macroscopic constraints, weighted by their probability.

The central question: *what is the probability of each microstate?* This is where ensembles come in.

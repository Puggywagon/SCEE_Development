# Equation — Induced Dipole Moment

**Topic:** See connections below
**Tags:** #polarisation #equation
**Status:** #to-verify

---

## Equation

$$
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{ind}} = \boldsymbol{\alpha} \cdot \mathbf{E}
$$

---

## Term Breakdown

| Symbol | Meaning | Units |
|--------|---------|-------|
| $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{ind}}$ | Induced dipole moment vector | C m or Debye |
| $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ | Polarisability tensor | C² s² kg⁻¹ or Å³ |
| $\mathbf{E}$ | Applied electric field vector | V m⁻¹ |

---

## Physical Interpretation

For small fields, the induced dipole moment is proportional to the applied electric field, with polarisability as the proportionality constant. In general polarisability is a tensor — the response differs along different molecular axes. For many thermodynamic purposes an isotropic average polarisability is sufficient.
